## 2. Decisions (coordinator)

1. **Reference values.** The plan says "compared with the ledger values". The ledger prints doubles at 15 significant
   digits and has no E0[N] or E1[N]. Layer 3 takes the counts from the ledger and theta, objective, E0[N] and E1[N] from
   the run's own rds (`history_combined` for BATON, `run_state.rds$history` for GA), which hold the full doubles. Theta
   from the ledger would simulate a design that differs from the stored one in the last bits.
2. **Comparison rule.** `power_count` and `type1_count` as integers, and `identical()` on objective, E0[N] and E1[N]
   (objective only for GA, which stores no E0[N] or E1[N]). This is stricter than layer 2's 4 decimals and is possible
   because the stored values are full doubles.
3. **Call path.** E2c's, not layer 2's: `parse_warmstart_scenarios` on `cohortB_multiarm_production.csv` (layer 2 used
   `parse_single_scenario`, inventory I2), E2c's environment block (no `BO_N_INIT`, no `BO_SEED`, the BATON block only on
   BATON rows), 16 workers (inventory I8), `--constraint=amd` as the E2c search jobs had.
4. **Candidates.** C1 PB-baton-1001 eval 1 (K 8), C2 PB-baton-1003 eval 95 (K 15, unsnapped), C3 PB-ga-1001 eval 274
   (K 15, comparator path `problem$simulate`). Values in reconnaissance Section 6.
5. **Local gate first.** All three run locally through `lib/ref` and `lib/dev_l2` (the 1c91871 build layer 2 used,
   `EngineCppSHA` in its DESCRIPTION, while `lib/dev` is an earlier build without the field) at 16 workers before anything is staged.
   `ref` must reproduce the stored values (this checks the recipe, as I5 did for layer 2, R 4.6.0 against 4.5.0), then
   `dev` must too. Local cost is about 3 minutes of reference wall. If local `ref` fails, stop: the recipe is wrong and
   Longleaf would prove nothing.
6. **No Longleaf `ref` control.** The stored rows are the reference, and they were produced on Longleaf.

## 4. Core-hour estimate

Reference walls of the three evaluations at 16 workers are 95.8, 40.7 and 40.4 s. Layer 2 measured the C++ engine on
this scenario at about 4 to 16 s for R 10,000 and 19 workers, and billed about 0.095 core-hours per task including
start-up. Three tasks at 16 CPUs: **about 0.3 core-hours**, at most 0.5 if start-up dominates. The 15-minute limit caps
the array at 12 core-hours if every task hung. The plan's bound is under 1.
