## 1. What phase 2 changes

1. **Exact posterior probabilities.** No posterior draws remain on any supported path.
   - Between-arm (vs-reference, PH model). Every decision reads only `logHR`, which the phase 1 engine draws as
     `R::rnorm(bmean, bsd)` with `bmean` and `bsd` from `ph_beta_mode_var_cpp`. The gamma draws and the two medians that
     follow are evaluated only for their error conditions (`simulate_chunk.cpp:430-473`). So `mean(logHR >= m)` is a
     Monte Carlo estimate of `pnorm(m, bmean, bsd, lower = FALSE)`, and `mean(logHR < m)` of `pnorm(m, bmean, bsd)`. The
     phase 2 engine uses these closed forms and draws nothing.
   - Single-arm (HC). The decision reads `mean(med < thr)` or `mean(med > thr)`, where `med` is the piecewise-exponential
     median of independent hazards `lambda_k ~ Gamma(alpha_k + E_k, rate beta_k + PT_k)`. The median is below `thr`
     exactly when the cumulative hazard at `thr` exceeds `log 2`. So `Pr(med < thr) = Pr(sum_k w_k lambda_k > log 2)`,
     with `w_k` the length of interval `k` that lies below `thr` (the last interval extends past the last cutpoint, as
     in `median_piecewise_r` and `calculate_median_survival_matrix_cpp`). Ties have probability zero, so
     `Pr(med > thr) = 1 - Pr(med < thr)`. This is the upper tail of a positively weighted sum of independent gammas.
     One interval with positive weight gives `R::pgamma` directly. Two or more use one-dimensional numerical inversion
     of the characteristic function (Gil-Pelaez) to absolute error 1e-9 or better, with the method and its error
     control chosen by the implementer and documented.
2. **Per-trial counter-based random numbers.** Patient-level draws (arrival gaps, arm assignment, event times) come
   from a generator seeded by `(scenario seed, trial index)` through a mixing function (splitmix64 seeding xoshiro256++,
   or an equivalent documented choice). Exponential and Weibull by inversion with R's parameterization
   (`rweibull(shape, scale)` is `scale * (-log U)^(1 / shape)`), arm assignment with the same probabilities as
   `sample(..., prob = )`. Dropout off draws nothing for dropout. Results then depend only on the seed and the trial
   index, never on the worker count or the chunking.
3. **Driver.** One draw of R's generator per scenario call gives the scenario seed, so `set.seed()` before a call still
   reproduces it. Trials go to workers in small blocks dispatched dynamically (`mc.preschedule = FALSE` or equivalent),
   per-trial outcomes come back, and every sum is taken in trial-index order. The returned list has the same names and
   meaning as the phase 1 chunk list, so `aggregate_results`, `run_scenarios` and BATON see no change.
4. **Incremental bookkeeping** (conditional, step P2-4). Only if profiling after steps P2-1 to P2-3 shows look slicing
   at 30% or more of R01 or R23 time. Constraint: on the same random stream, integer counts identical and every double
   within relative 1e-12 of the re-slicing code, checked by a test that runs both.

Selection. Option `evolveTrial.engine`, values `"exact"` (phase 2) and `"cpp"` (phase 1). The default stays `"cpp"`, so
the engine of record and the frozen JASA stack are unchanged unless a caller opts in. `evolveTrial.use_cpp_engine =
FALSE` still selects the R body. The phase 2 engine supports the phase 1 supported set and stops outside it, never
falling back.

## 3. Gates (prespecified 2026-11-03 night, before any phase 2 run)

- **G1, exact probabilities.** Single-arm: on at least 2,000 random inputs covering 1 to 8 positive weights, event
  counts 0 to 300, and thresholds inside, at and beyond the cutpoints, the exact tail agrees with an independent
  reference (R `integrate` of the conditional `pgamma` for two weights, a long-double Moschopoulos series where it
  converges, and Monte Carlo with 10^7 draws for at least 50 cases) to absolute 1e-7 against the deterministic
  references and within 4 Monte Carlo standard errors against the draws. Between-arm: the closed form against `pnorm` on
  the phase 1 Laplace output, and against 10^6 phase 1 draws for 50 cases within 4 standard errors.
- **G3, determinism.** For every one of the 10 acceptance designs at R = 2,000: `identical()` results at 1, 4, 16 and 19
  workers and across two block sizes, and a different scenario seed gives a different result.
- **G4, no regression.** The evolveTrial test suite passes (phase 1 count was 723 passed, 0 failed, 33 skipped), and
  phase 1 layer 1 (`dev/perf/harness/run_layer1.R`) still reports 0 mismatches, since the phase 1 engine must be
  untouched.
- **G5, statistical equivalence** against `dev/perf/evidence/acceptance_set_reference_rows.csv`. For each of the 10
  designs, run the phase 2 engine through the certify call path (the layer 2 runner with the phase 2 option) at every
  reference `(design, seed)` row, R = 10,000 per seed, 19 workers. Pool counts over seeds for both engines, so pooled R
  is 50,000 or 100,000 per design for both. Then, per design:
  1. Power and type I error. Two one-sided tests on the difference of proportions (phase 2 minus reference), unpooled
     standard error, each one-sided test at level 0.05, margins 0.01 for power and 0.005 for type I error. Equivalent
     when both one-sided tests reject.
  2. `EN_null` and `EN_alt`. Two one-sided tests on the difference of means at level 0.05, margin 1% of the reference
     value, standard error from the per-trial variance of the phase 2 run applied to both samples (the reference rows
     store no variance).
  3. Classification. Pooled power on the same side of 0.80 and pooled type I error on the same side of 0.10 for both
     engines.
  G5 passes when every design passes all three. The margins are not changed after any result is seen. A failure is
  reported with a paired diagnostic (on fixed slices, how often the exact rule and the Monte Carlo rule disagree), not
  re-run until it passes.
- **G6, timing.** Seconds per 1,000 trials for R01 and R23 at 1 and 16 workers, with the step 7 timing scripts, phase 1
  (`dev_l2`) and phase 2 (`dev_p2`) on the same quiet machine. Target: R23 under 8 s per 1,000 trials at one worker
  (the engine-of-record R body measured 75.1 s in the design baseline), and 16-worker efficiency above phase 1's.

## 4. Commits

One commit per step that passes its gates, message carrying the gate result. Nothing is pushed and nothing is installed
outside `dev/perf/lib` (handoff item 2 stays with the PI).

## 5. Stop conditions

The run stops and reports, rather than improvising, when G1 cannot be met by a documented method, when G3 or G4 fails
after one diagnosis and fix attempt, when G5 fails, or when any step would touch the frozen JASA stack, the installed
engine, the phase 1 engine's behavior or Longleaf.

## 6. Decisions made without the PI (for review)

1. The phase 2 engine is opt-in, phase 1 stays the default.
2. Gil-Pelaez inversion for the single-arm tail, with `pgamma` for one positive weight.
3. Counter-based seeding through splitmix64 and xoshiro256++ (or a documented equivalent), inversion samplers.
4. The G5 margins (0.01 power, 0.005 type I error, 1% expected sample size). The design said "bounds at the
   certification margins" without numbers. These are about 2.5 and 1.7 standard errors of a single certification seed at R = 10,000 (about 4 to 8 pooled standard errors at R = 50,000 to 100,000).
5. Gates run locally, because Longleaf needs an interactive login.
6. With exact integration no posterior draw count survives, so design Section 6's "draw count as a fidelity setting"
   becomes moot for the supported set.
